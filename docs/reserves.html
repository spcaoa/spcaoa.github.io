How ₹11.76 crore became ₹9.33 Cr

Jun 2022
Owners' corpus of ₹11.76 Cr collected by Sobha at handover. For sixteen months Sobha ran the estate out of it: ₹1.88 Cr of maintenance was charged to the corpus while interest of ₹1.17 Cr was credited.
Nov 2023 – Jun 2024
Sobha returned ₹9.30 Cr to the association in nine instalments. The association placed it in fixed deposits at HDFC and ICICI. Sobha kept ₹91 L, which it treats as security for its facility-management service.
FY 2024–25 and 2025–26
Interest was spent on running costs, which is reasonable; ₹50 L of principal was also used in FY 25–26, which was not approved by the General Body. The Sub-Committee's report sets out the withdrawals.
Today
₹9.33 Cr, of which ₹8.58 Cr is in bank deposits. A bridge from the audited balance sheets, showing every rupee of the difference, will be in the meeting pack.

The rule the committee proposes

  1. Interest is income; principal is not. Interest is budgeted and spent on running costs. Principal is not withdrawn without a General Body resolution, and every withdrawal is reported here the month it happens.
  2. Two signatures for every deposit movement, one of them not an office-bearer, as the Sub-Committee recommended.
  3. A monthly statement of the deposits on these pages, from the banks' summaries, not from the books.
  4. A sinking fund, from next year, so that the ₹50 L used is rebuilt and the big repairs of the next ten years (lifts, painting, waterproofing) have a plan.
