Where we stand

Running Sobha Palm Court costs about ₹30 L a month. Maintenance brings in about ₹19 L. The rest has been coming out of the reserves — the money owners paid at handover, meant for big repairs. That cannot go on. These pages show the numbers and the funding options the Managing Committee worked out before it stepped down. What goes to owners next is for the new Committee to decide.

The short version

  1. Between April and August we spent ₹1.50 Cr. Maintenance for those five months was ₹96.1 L. The difference came from the reserves.
  2. The reserves are about ₹9.69 Cr. Their interest already goes on running costs — though about ₹34 L a year of it sits inside the ICICI deposits until they mature, so it is earned but not in the bank. Last year ₹50 L of the reserves themselves was used as well, and this year another ₹38 L so far.
  3. The Managing Committee worked out four ways to close the gap, from a 15% to a 39% increase. The options page shows what each would mean for your flat. None has been put to a vote.
  4. On 3 October the Managing Committee resigned. It stays on as a caretaker until a new Committee takes charge, and no later than 15 November 2026. The Returning Officer has been asked to hold elections.
  5. Maintenance stays at the current rate. Only the General Body can change it, and what to put to owners is for the new Committee to decide.
  6. Last year's accounts were reviewed by the Finance Sub-Committee, a group of resident owners. Their report, the previous Managing Committee's answers and the auditor's clarifications are on the documents page. The ₹48 lakh shortfall last year was reported at the July AGM.
